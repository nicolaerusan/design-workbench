Why now

A lot of software now starts as a conversation. You describe a screen to Claude or ChatGPT, it writes the code, and you look at what came out.

Design tools haven’t caught up. They live in a separate app with a separate copy of your components, and the bridge to your agent is screenshots and copy-paste. Ask for three directions and two of them are gone by the next message.

DesignBench keeps every direction. It renders your real React components, saves each variant as a file next to the original, and lays them out on one canvas. What you compare is what ships.

Every permutation, on one bench

Designs are the different takes on a component. States are the situations it has to handle, like hover, loading and disabled. Properties are the knobs you’d find in an inspector. DesignBench crosses all three, so you see every combination and spot the ones nobody designed.

How it works

  1. Ask your agent to set it up.

    It installs DesignBench, finds your components and writes previews with realistic props.

    npx designbench init

  2. Ask for directions.

    “Five takes on the empty state, one of them weird.” Each variant is a copy of the source file under .design-workbench/ideas, so your app stays untouched.

  3. Compare on the canvas.

    Every direction renders with the same props, state and viewport, at 375, 768 or 1280 px. Point at what you like, leave a note, and go another round.

  4. Promote the one you pick.

    One command shows the diff against your source. Add --apply to write it, with a backup kept beside the variant.

    npx designbench variant promote empty-state weird --apply

What is available

DesignBench 0.3 is an early preview for React projects. It runs as a local workbench next to your app. Claude Code, Codex and Cursor drive it through a small CLI and an AGENT.md guide it writes into your repo.

You get a searchable component library, saved variants, side-by-side comparison, editable properties and states, links that reopen an exact selection, and guarded promotion back into your source.

What is next

The bench inside the chat. We’re bringing the canvas into Claude and ChatGPT conversations as an app, so you can point at a variant and ask for changes without leaving the thread.

After that comes Pattern Garden, a library of real components from across the web that you can start new variants from.

What it costs

DesignBench is free and MIT licensed. There’s no account and no model API key. Your agent does the coding on the plan you already have.

Start in your agent

Open your React project in Claude Code, Codex or Cursor and paste one line. Your agent reads the setup guide and does the rest:

  1. Checks the project fits: React 18.2 or 19, and Node 20.19+ or 22.12+.
  2. Installs DesignBench as a dev dependency and creates a .design-workbench/ folder.
  3. Fits it to your app: your global styles and providers, and previews with real content for the 5 to 8 components you use most.
  4. Opens every preview to check it renders, then sends you a link to the bench, running on your machine at 127.0.0.1:7070.

It only changes .design-workbench/, package.json and your lockfile, and it doesn’t commit anything. You can read the guide your agent follows.
